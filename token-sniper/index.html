Verdict & thoughts — 9 Nov 2026

Muse's honest assessment of the strategy, given to the user today — kept on the record alongside the numbers.

  1. The rules are right. Fees counted both ways, safety first on every contract, never reduce the portfolio — exactly correct for a ~$5 bankroll. One careless interaction wipes it out at this size, and you can't know whether you're growing without net-of-fees math. Those three are non-negotiable.
  2. The tension. Sniper-only plus never-reduce plus $0.02 a trade means the engine barely fires — zero buys in two days — and gas eats a quarter to half of each trade. Be clear-eyed: the sniper is a lottery-ticket machine, not a compounder. It only works if it hits one big winner, and that takes a lot of small shots over time. The min-500 → 250 loosening helps a little, but it doesn't change that math.
  3. One reservation. Pausing the airdrop check removes the only thing putting anything in the wallet without paying for it — free capital is the one thing that actually grows a tiny portfolio without risk. The "anything, anywhere, anytime" rule is the safety valve: if a genuinely free and positive opportunity appears, it will still be flagged rather than passed over in silence.

The verdict: "Good rules, right focus, but expect slow — the sniper needs one big hit, and that's a matter of time and volume, not effort."

How it works

Four parts. The watcher never sleeps as a background process — a scheduled job runs it every 2 minutes, so it survives machine replacements. State lives in a file, not in memory.

  1. 01 · WATCH — Watcher (run.js): Scans the Uniswap v4 PoolManager for new Initialize events since the last scanned block. A new pool = a new launch to check.
  2. 02 · CHECK — Evaluator (evaluate.js): Runs all six legit checks below — plus, in v2/v3, the score, the token-age gate and the minimum-tokens rule (250 since 9 Nov). Read-only — it sends no transactions, so dry-run mode is completely safe.
  3. 03 · BUY — Executor (trade.js): Buys via the proven Robinhood v4 swap route: wrap ETH → WETH, approve through Permit2, swap through the Universal Router. Bypasses the v4 quoter, which reverts on hooked pools.
  4. 04 · MANAGE — Position manager: Reads each held pool's current price on every run. Sells 50% at +100%, everything at −50%, otherwise holds and logs the decision.

The live gate: Live buying is controlled by one flag — state.live in STATE.json. The scheduled watcher can pass "live" all day, but the bot stays in dry-run until that flag is true, and it only became true after an explicit "Go". Setting it back to false stops all buys and sells while scanning continues.

The six legit checks

Every check must pass before v1 bought (v1 is now retired). One failure = skip, with the reason logged. This is the whole point of the bot — most launches fail, and that's correct. Note: the token-age gate and the minimum-tokens rule (500 at launch, loosened to 250 on 9 Nov) are v2/v3 only — v1 never carried them, which is part of why it was retired.

  1. WETH pair only — One side of the pool must be WETH. In practice only about 1 in 9 new Robinhood pools qualifies — the rest are mostly native-ETH pairs.
  2. Not our own token — Never buys $MUSE or $RMUSE, and never buys a token the wallet already holds from an earlier snipe.
  3. Standard ERC-20 — Name, symbol, decimals and total supply must all be readable onchain.
  4. No dangerous functions — Bytecode is scanned for mint, pause, blacklist, max-transaction, max-wallet, tax / fee setters, and trading switches.
  5. Honeypot simulation — Simulates buying, then selling everything. If the sell reverts or returns under 50% of the input, it's a skip. If the quoter reverts because of a hook, a transfer simulation is used instead — a revert alone is not a fail.
  6. Real liquidity, right now — The pool must have active liquidity at the current price. A pool can be created empty — FLOPPY was empty at first check, then had liquidity minutes later. The check caught both states correctly.

Hard lessons from v1

  1. A quoter revert proves nothing. Our own $MUSE and $RMUSE pools revert the official v4 quoter because of their fee hook. Treating a revert as "honeypot" would reject every hooked pool, including legitimate ones. The fix is the transfer-simulation fallback.
  2. Receipt polling can throw. The first live buy (SLIGNAL, ~08:49 WIB) failed because the library throws when a transaction isn't mined yet, instead of returning "pending". The fix is raw receipt polling that keeps waiting.
  3. Don't trust the log filter. On the Robinhood RPC, the library's log call silently ignored the topic filter and returned hundreds of wrong events. The bot now uses raw eth_getLogs and verifies the event topic itself.
